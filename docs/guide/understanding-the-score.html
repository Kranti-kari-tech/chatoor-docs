Understanding the score

The big number​

The score runs from -100 (very weak) to +100 (very strong).

ScoreLabelMeaning
+25 or higherPositive signalsThe numbers look good overall
between -25 and +25Mixed signalsNothing clear either way
-25 or lowerNegative signalsThe numbers look weak overall

:::info A description, not a decision The label says what the data shows today. It doesn't say what will happen or what you should do. Read the reasons and the bear case, check the track record shown with every score, and make your own decision. :::

Special cases:

  • Positive signals, earnings soon. The score is positive, but the company reports results within 14 days. Results can move a stock 10% or more overnight in either direction, so the label flags it.
  • (thin data). Less than 40% of the data was available, e.g. for crypto. Trust it less.

The seven factors​

Each factor gets a mini-score from -1 (bad) to +1 (good). They're blended using weights:

FactorWeightWhat it checksGood sign
Valuation15P/E ratio and PEG: how expensive the stock is compared with its profitsLow P/E (around 12 or less)
Growth15How fast sales and profits are growing25%+ sales growth
Profitability15Profit margin and return on equity25%+ margins
Health10Debt, short-term cash cushion, free cash flowLow debt, positive cash flow
Momentum20Price trend vs. moving averages, RSI, and performance vs. the marketPrice above its 200-day average, beating the market
Analysts15Professional analysts' ratings and price targetsStrong buy ratings, target well above price
Earnings10Did the company beat profit forecasts recently?Beat expectations 4 quarters in a row

New to these terms? See the Glossary.

Data: complete, partial or thin​

How much of the data the score needs was available. It is not a measure of how likely the score is to be right.

  • Complete: 85%+ of the data was available.
  • Partial: 60-85%.
  • Thin: under 60%. Factors with no data are left out, not counted as zero.

Flags​

FlagMeaning
⚠ Earnings in N daysBig move expected either way
⚠ OverboughtRSI over 70 and near the 52-week high, so it may have run too far, too fast
⚠ High leverageDebt is more than twice the company's equity
⚠ Loss-makingThe company lost money over the past year
⚠ Fewer than 3 analystsToo little professional coverage to trust the analyst factor

ETFs and funds​

An ETF (like GLD, SPY or NIFTYBEES.NS) is a basket of investments, not a company. It has no profits or analyst ratings, so it gets its own five factors:

FactorWeightWhat it checksGood sign
Momentum35Price trend, RSI, performance vs. its comparison indexPrice above its 200-day average
Track record15Average yearly return over 3 and 5 years12-15%+ a year
Risk20How much the price swings, and how far it is below its 52-week highCalm, close to its high
Cost15Expense ratio (the yearly fee), compared with similar fundsUnder 0.10% a year
Liquidity15Fund size, and whether its price matches the value of what it holds (NAV)Billions in assets, price within 0.2% of NAV

What each fund is compared against depends on what it holds:

Fund holdsCompared against
Shares (SPY, QQQ, NIFTYBEES.NS)Its market index (S&P 500, Nifty 50)
US bonds (TLT, AGG)AGG, the US bond market
Gold, silver, oil (GLD, GOLDBEES.NS)Nothing: judged on its own return
Crypto (IBIT)Bitcoin

Extra ETF warnings:

FlagMeaning
⚠ Leveraged or inverse fund2x, 3x or "short" funds reset daily and lose value over long holds
⚠ Bear market20% or more below its 52-week high
⚠ High expense ratioFees above 0.75% a year
⚠ Small fundUnder 100M in assets: more likely to close, wider buy/sell gap
⚠ Premium / discount to NAVThe price is more than 2% away from the value of its holdings

:::note Indian ETFs Yahoo Finance has no fund data (fees, size, track record) for Indian ETFs such as GOLDBEES.NS. They are scored on price trend and risk only, with thin data. Check the expense ratio on the fund house's website. :::

Can I change the weights?​

Yes. Just ask Claude, for example: "I care less about analyst opinions, lower that weight to 5." ETF weights work the same way, for example "for ETFs, care more about cost". Your changes are saved in ~/.stock-analyst/config.json and survive updates. Claude may also suggest changes after reviews.
