Dashboard

Prefer clicking to typing? The skill includes a small web page that runs on your own computer. Type in symbols, press Scan, and each result appears as a card.

Start here​

New to the tool? The dashboard opens on Start, a six-step path (its example companies match your computer's time zone: Indian ones in India, UK ones in the UK, US ones elsewhere): know what the tool is, analyse a company you know, read its analysis (especially the bear case), track your first idea with your own view, learn three key terms, and come back for your first review after 30 days. Steps tick themselves off as you do them. Once the first five are done, the Start tab steps aside and the dashboard opens on the Scanner. To see it again, open Learn → Open the Start guide.

The Trading desk and Portfolio tabs are off for new users. Turn the Trading desk on from Start → Day trading tools, after a short checklist, and Portfolio from Start → Track what you own. Hide either again with Hide tab. If you already had holdings, Portfolio stays on.

Open it​

The easiest way is to ask Claude:

Open the stock dashboard

Or start it yourself in a terminal:

uv run ~/.claude/skills/stock-analyst/scripts/dashboard.py --open

Your browser opens http://127.0.0.1:8787. Leave the terminal open while you use it, and press Ctrl + C in the terminal to stop it.

The Chatoor mark at the top left, a white trishul on violet, takes you back to Start from anywhere.

:::tip One command for everything Add --auto to keep your portfolio and watchlist updated every 30 minutes and have reports written automatically with a free AI:

uv run ~/.claude/skills/stock-analyst/scripts/dashboard.py --open --auto

See Free AI reports to set up a free key. :::

Scanner​

  1. Type a symbol (like AAPL, GLD or RELIANCE.NS) and press Enter. It becomes a chip. Add as many as you like, up to 20.
  2. Press Scan.
  3. Each stock or ETF gets a card showing:
    • its price and 6-month change
    • the score gauge and label (Positive, Mixed or Negative signals)
    • the track record: how often the tool's past calls with that label turned out right
    • up to three plain-English points from its strongest factors, like "Very profitable" or "Expensive for its profits" (click one to learn what it means)
    • the seven factor bars behind Show the 7 factors, which show exactly what pushed the score up or down. Tick Show factor details above the cards to see them on every card; the dashboard remembers your choice
    • warnings, such as upcoming earnings or a bear market
  4. Click a card to open its full analysis (see below).

Filter the stocks you follow (above the cards) picks out, from the stocks you follow, the ones that fit what you're looking for, using the analysis panel's own answers. Scanning shows every stock you add; this narrows them down. For example, a positive data label, a rising trend and results within 14 days shows which of your stocks to read up on before their results. Conditions: the data label, the business, the price, the trend, how far below its 52-week high, RSI under 30, or a report within 14 days. Add other symbols to screen them too. Click a match to open its analysis. Find matches lists them. Save and alert me keeps the filter and checks it every 15 minutes while the dashboard is open: a message (and a desktop notification, if you allow them) says when a stock starts or stops matching. A filter narrows what to read next; it doesn't say what to do.

Above the cards, Results coming up lists the stocks in your watchlist and portfolio that report in the next two weeks. Click one to open its analysis at The next report. 5. Press Track this idea to save it to your journal. You say what you expect over the next 30 days (beat, match or lag the index) and how sure you are (50% to 100%), tick a short checklist, and can add your reason in one line. The box doesn't repeat the tool's label, so you decide for yourself; once you've saved, a message shows your call next to the tool's and whether you agree. After 30 days the Journal shows how both turned out. 6. Guess first (above the cards): tick it to hide the tool's score, label and summary. Cards show only the numbers, with Make my call; hover over any number's name (P/E, ROE, RSI…) to see what it means. Once you've made your call, or pressed Show the tool's view, that card shows the tool's view as usual. Seeing a score first tends to pull your own view towards it; this shows whether your judgement adds anything. The dashboard remembers the setting.

Your list of symbols is saved automatically, so next time the dashboard scans them straight away. Use Sort by to order cards by score, momentum or symbol.

Options view​

The Stocks | Options switch at the top of the search box scans the same symbols for their options instead. Each card shows:

  • the move needed by the next expiry about a week away, such as ±4.2%, and the price range it implies, from a call and a put at the strike closest to today's price
  • three reads on what option prices say: whether they're pricey, about fair or cheap next to the stock's recent swings, whether an event such as earnings seems priced in, and whether traders are paying extra for protection against a fall

Cards have no score and no label: they describe what option prices imply, not whether to buy or sell. Click a card to open the full options panel in the side panel, the same one as on the Trading desk, with the strike and expiry pickers, the table, the scenario and the history. Open the stock analysis switches to the stock's usual panel.

Yahoo has option prices for US-listed symbols only, so Indian and London symbols show "No options data". Funds, crypto and many small companies have no listed options. The view covers your first 10 symbols, because each one is a separate lookup at Yahoo. The dashboard remembers which view you used last.

The analysis panel​

Click any card and a panel slides in from the right. Press Esc, the close button, or click outside it to close it.

Each fact appears once: for example, RSI is on its gauge and not repeated in the numbers below. After you scan, the dashboard fetches what the panel needs in the background, so opening a card is quick.

:::tip Want the full report section filled in? Press Write report with free AI at the bottom of the panel (see Free AI reports), or ask Claude in Claude Code: deep dive on GLD for a printable report. Either way the report is saved and appears in the panel. :::

:::caution About the entry and exit levels They come from one simple rule, the same for every stock. In an uptrend (the 50-day average above the 200-day, and the price above both) it waits for a pullback to the nearest support (the 50-day or 200-day average, or the 20-day low), with the stop one normal day's move (ATR) below it. Without an uptrend it doesn't plan a pullback: on past prices, those lost money. In a downtrend it waits for a close back above the 50-day average. The rule doesn't know about earnings, news or the business. When you track an idea, its levels are saved with it, and the Journal's Rule column shows whether the price reached the first target or the stop first. A day that touched both counts as the stop, and a trade that hasn't finished after 90 days counts as neither. The line above the table adds these up, so you can see how the rule does over time. Use it as a starting point to check against the case against and the headlines; the decision is yours. :::

:::note Where the "why" comes from The panel's explanations are built from price and company data, so they describe what is happening (for example "in a downtrend, 26% below its high"). The reasons behind a move, like a news event or a rate decision, come from the headlines and from Claude's full report. :::

Trading desk​

The Trading desk shows today's key levels, volume, the market and your own risk limits, with a position size calculator. See Trading desk.

Paper trade​

The Paper trade tab is a pretend-money account: buy and sell at today's prices, see how you do against the market, and see how your tracked ideas are doing next to each holding. See Paper trade with pretend money.

Portfolio​

The Portfolio tab tracks what you really own. It's off until you turn it on from Start → Track what you own.

  • Add a holding: type the symbol, how many you bought, and the price you paid for each, then press Add holding. Bought more of the same stock later? Add it again; the average cost is worked out for you.
  • Totals per currency: your value, the amount invested and your profit or loss, shown separately for dollars, rupees and so on. Nothing is converted between currencies.
  • Allocation bar: how much of your money each holding makes up.
  • Holdings table: quantity, average cost, today's price, value, profit or loss, weight, and today's score for each holding. A concentrated warning appears when one holding is more than 30% of its currency's total.
  • Click a holding to open its full analysis, the same panel as in the Scanner.
  • Sold some? Press the bin icon, type how many you sold (or leave it as "all"), and press Remove.
  • Refresh prices fetches the latest prices and scores.

It's the same portfolio Claude manages when you say "I bought 10 AAPL at 180", so both stay in sync.

Journal​

The Journal tab lists every call, whether it was logged from the dashboard or by Claude:

  • the price then and now, and the return since, with the index over the same time under it (long reasons are cut to two lines; hover to read them in full)
  • how the comparison index did over the same time
  • the outcome: right, wrong, or too early (calls are checked after 30 days)
  • your overall hit rate
  • Look back on each checked idea: it shows what you expected and why, next to what happened, and asks whether the result came from your reasons or from luck. Your answer and what you'd do differently are saved to your lessons, which Claude reads before every analysis

Rewind​

Practice on the past with a result in seconds: a mystery stock from years ago, your call, then the reveal. See Rewind.

Today: your daily digest​

On the first launch each day (once you've done the basics on Start), Chatoor opens Today: a calm look at the day before you start. It's always under the newspaper icon at the top right.

  • The market: the main indexes and how they've moved today.
  • Your stocks: each one you hold or watch, with its last saved price, the change since the scan before, and its data label. Start your day takes you to the Scanner for fresh prices.
  • Worth a look today: ideas old enough to look back on, results coming up, and your paper account.
  • News that may matter: headlines from Google News, the GDELT news index, the Federal Reserve and SEC filings, ranked by what they're about (results, interest rates, inflation, trade, deals, legal trouble, analyst changes, leadership), whether they mention your stocks, how widely they're covered and how recent they are. Each says in plain words why it may matter. Many headlines about one event show as the top two, with a count of the rest. Headlines are the outlets' own words, not Chatoor's view.
  • Most traded today: Yahoo's busiest stocks, with the data label if you've scanned one. Check it yourself scans it, because busy isn't the same as good.
  • One idea for today: a short, sourced quote from a well-known investor or trader, with a question that turns it into practice, and a link to Learn.

Untick Show this each morning to stop it opening by itself.

Your profile​

The person icon at the top right opens your profile:

  • Whose copy this is: your email, the version running, when your key ends, the day you started, and the folder your data is in.
  • Your record: how often your own calls were right (where you gave your view), next to the tool on the same ideas; your Rewind rounds; your paper trade return; and, if you use the Trading desk, how many of your logged trades had a stop written down. Until there are 5 calls or rounds you see counts ("3 of 4"), not percentages, because a rate from a handful is mostly luck.
  • The last 30 days and the 30 before: ideas tracked, Rewind rounds and how often you were right in Rewind, so you can see whether you're practising more and improving.

It compares you with yourself and with the tool, and it reads only files on your computer: nothing is sent anywhere.

If you take part in a study, the profile also shows How you compare with other participants once your final check-in has reached the researcher and at least 20 participants have finished. For four learning measures (your quiz change, Rewind, how sure you were against how often you were right, and your calls against the tool's) a grey band shows where the middle half of finished participants fall, with you marked on it. There are no ranks, and money is never compared. The study database sends back only the group's band and your own values, never anyone else's.

Learn​

The Learn tab explains every number and label in plain English, one topic at a time: using the tool, the score, stock factors, fund factors, price and trend, day trading, risk, your results and data limits. Each card starts with one short sentence, many have a small diagram (the score scale, moving averages, RSI, the 52-week range, the comparison index, position size, R, stops, the track record and how sure vs. how right), and More opens the full explanation. Search looks across every topic. Hover over a factor name, a plain-words point or any small i icon anywhere in the dashboard to see that card's short line and diagram in a see-through preview; click to jump straight to the full card on Learn.

Good to know​

  • It's private. The dashboard only accepts connections from your own computer. Other devices and websites can't reach it.
  • Every scan fetches the latest prices. Cards appear one by one as each stock comes back, and each card says when its price is from ("Price at 3:55 pm", in the exchange's own time). Refresh prices above the cards fetches them again. Prices come from Yahoo Finance and can be delayed by about 15 minutes. If Yahoo's daily list hasn't caught up with the latest session yet, the latest 5-minute price is used instead, so the price and the 1, 3 and 6-month changes stay current.
  • Fetched data is saved on your computer in ~/.stock-analyst/cache.db. Other parts of the app reuse it for 5 minutes (headlines: 15), and if Yahoo Finance doesn't respond you see the last saved data, marked as such. Each stock's daily score is kept too, building up a history.
  • Just updated the skill? Stop the dashboard with Ctrl + C, start it again, and refresh the page.
  • Empty pages explain themselves. Before you've scanned, tracked or logged anything, each page shows a short explanation and a button for what to do next.
  • If something goes wrong, you see a plain message where it happened, never a wall of technical text: what couldn't load, with Try again and Report a problem. If the server goes away you get a "The dashboard has stopped" page with how to restart it, and a banner if parts of the page couldn't load from the internet. The technical details go to a log file on your computer (~/.stock-analyst/logs/dashboard.log) and into problem reports. See Troubleshooting.
  • Port already in use? Start it on another port with --port 8788.
  • Narrow window? On a phone-sized screen the tabs move to a bar at the bottom, with an icon for each, so every section is one tap away.
  • Light or dark? It follows your system setting. Use the button in the top-right corner to switch.
