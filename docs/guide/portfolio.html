Portfolio tracker

Keep track of what you own and how it's doing.

:::tip Prefer clicking? The dashboard has a Portfolio tab with the same holdings, plus an allocation bar and today's score for each one. :::

Add what you bought​

Just tell Claude:

I bought 10 shares of AAPL at 180
Add 5 RELIANCE.NS at 1250 to my portfolio

Bought the same stock again at a different price? Add it again. The tracker works out your average cost automatically.

See how you're doing​

Show my portfolio
ticker      qty   avg cost     price      value     P&L   weight
AAPL         10     180.00    346.48   3,464.85   92.5%   76.8%  ⚠ concentrated
MSFT          2     400.00    524.48   1,048.96   31.1%   23.2%

Total value 4,513.81 · cost 3,300.00 · P&L +36.8%
  • P&L is profit or loss as a percentage.
  • Weight is how much of your portfolio this stock makes up.
  • ⚠ concentrated means one stock is more than 30% of your portfolio. If it falls sharply, your whole portfolio suffers.

Sold something?​

I sold 5 AAPL
I sold all my MSFT

:::note Mixed currencies Each stock is shown in its own currency (USD for AAPL, INR for RELIANCE.NS). If you hold stocks from different countries, the total adds the numbers as-is without converting currencies, so treat it as rough. :::

:::info Your holdings are private Your portfolio is saved only on your computer, in ~/.stock-analyst/portfolio.json. It is never uploaded anywhere. The tracker isn't connected to your broker, so it only knows what you tell it. :::

Paper trade with pretend money​

New to investing? Try things out first without risking anything. In the dashboard, open the Paper trade tab. Ask Claude: start a practice account with 10000, practice buy 10 NVDA, practice sell all NVDA.

  • Start with pretend cash (10,000 by default) in USD, INR or GBP. One currency per account, so totals never mix.
  • Buy and sell at the dashboard's current prices, with no fees. Leave the shares empty to sell everything.
  • Each buy remembers what the tool said at that moment, so you can see later how buying against its label worked out.
  • See how you're doing: total value, profit or loss against your pretend money, how the market (SPY, the Nifty 50 or the FTSE 100) did since you started, a chart of the account by day, and plain observations such as "42% of the account is in NVDA".
  • Your ideas next to your holdings: each holding shows your latest tracked idea on that symbol, with your view and whether it is leaning right or wrong so far. No idea yet? Press Track this idea to open the analysis and track one. Ideas you track, below, lists your latest 10 with what the tool said at the time; the Journal has them all.
  • Options (US dollar accounts): switch the order panel to Options, type a US symbol, pick Call (gains if the stock rises) or Put (gains if it falls), an expiry date and a strike price, and how many contracts. Before you buy, it shows the cost (the ask × 100 shares × contracts), the price the stock must pass by expiry for the contract to pay, and the most you can lose: the whole cost. Buys fill at the ask and sales at the bid, as at a real broker. Option contracts lists what you hold, valued at the middle of the quote, with days to expiry and a Sell button. The day after expiry, a contract is settled at what it's worth then (how far the stock closed beyond the strike, or nothing). The first options order asks you to confirm two facts: an option can lose its whole cost, and each contract covers 100 shares. From the Scanner's Options view, Try it in Paper trade opens this form at the panel's strike and date. Only buying is possible: no selling options you don't hold, so no margin.
  • Reset any time. Your real holdings and journal are never touched.

Nothing is ever really bought or sold, and prices from Yahoo can be delayed. The Paper trade account is saved only on your computer, in ~/.stock-analyst/practice.json.
