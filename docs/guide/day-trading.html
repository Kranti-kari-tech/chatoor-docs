Trading desk

The dashboard's Trading desk is for watching stocks during the trading day. It shows today's key price levels, how busy each stock is, and your own risk limits, so you can plan a trade and size it before you place it with your broker.

:::caution Read this first

  • This tool never places trades. You place orders yourself, with your broker.
  • Yahoo prices can lag by a few minutes, and its live volume figures are sometimes wrong. Fine for planning, not for split-second decisions.
  • Levels and tags describe the chart. They are not buy or sell signals.
  • Most people who day trade lose money. The risk tools are there to keep losses small. :::

Open it​

Start the dashboard (see Dashboard) and click Trading desk in the top bar.

  • Every minute while the market, premarket or after hours is open, the tab refreshes in the background: your list, the selected stock's levels and chart, the options panel and any market list you have open. These use prices saved in the last minute. When the market is closed it checks every 5 minutes, so it notices premarket starting.
  • Refresh asks Yahoo for the latest prices straight away, skipping the saved copy. It reads "Refreshing…" until they arrive.
  • Coming back to the browser tab after a while refreshes at once.

The top of the tab​

  • US or India: the switch at the top picks which market the tab follows. On a computer set to India time it starts on India. It sets the session clock, the market bar and sectors, the time your trades are logged in, and when "today" starts for your limits.
  • Session clock: where the market is in its day. US: premarket, opening hour, lunch lull, power hour, after hours. India: pre-open (9:00 to 9:15 IST), opening hour, midday lull, last hour. It also shows how many minutes are left in that stretch. (premarket, opening hour, lunch lull, power hour, after hours) and how many minutes are left in that stretch.
  • Market bar: the S&P 500, Nasdaq 100 and Russell 2000 (through the SPY, QQQ and IWM funds), the VIX "fear gauge" and the 10-year Treasury yield.
  • Sectors today: sector funds sorted from strongest to weakest, so you can see where money is flowing.
  • For India the bar shows the Nifty 50, Bank Nifty and India VIX, and the sectors are NSE indices (IT, Auto, Pharma, FMCG, Metals, Energy, Realty, PSU banks, Infra). Top gainers, Top losers and Most active are hidden, because Yahoo's lists cover US stocks only, and so is the US pattern day trader rule.

Strategy lessons​

At the top of the tab, Strategy lessons teach one day-trading strategy at a time. Press Start the lessons: the first opens that day, and a new one every two weeks after it. Earlier lessons stay open to revisit.

  1. Opening range breakout: a move beyond the first 15 minutes' high or low.
  2. VWAP reclaim and rejection: the price crossing back over, or turning away from, the day's average price.
  3. Gap and go, and the gap fill: an open well above or below yesterday's close that follows through, or drifts back.
  4. Yesterday's high and low: breaks of yesterday's levels, and the many that fail.

Each lesson says what the strategy is, when it applies, where it would enter, exit if wrong and take profit (from the levels this tab shows), and what tends to go wrong. Practise in Paper trade opens the Paper trade order form on the stock you have selected, with the lesson attached. When you sell, the lesson shows Your practice: how many paper trades, how many made money, and the average result. Paper trade can only buy, so the falling versions of these strategies are for watching. The lessons are education, not signals: most people lose money day trading.

When you select a stock, Lesson strategies on that stock shows two things:

  • Today: for each strategy, whether the chart shows it now (for example "Trading above the 15-minute high, on 1.8x normal volume"), nearly, not yet, or not today.
  • The last 60 trading days: each strategy's buy side replayed on that stock's 5-minute prices, at most one trade a day: in at the close of the bar that sets it up, out at the stop, at twice the risk, or at the day's close. They're ranked by the average result, with how many trades each rests on; a rate shows from 5 trades. A few dozen day trades are mostly luck, so this is a record, not a forecast, and it's before costs.

Your list and market movers​

Your list is your portfolio plus the Scanner's watchlist. Each row shows:

ColumnWhat it means
ChangeToday's move from yesterday's close
GapHow far today's open was from yesterday's close
vs VWAPDistance from VWAP, the average price paid today weighted by volume. Many day traders treat it as the day's "fair price"
Rel. volVolume so far compared with a normal day at this time of day. 2x means twice as busy as usual
ATRAverage true range: how much the stock normally moves in a day (14-day average), in price and as a %
Range usedHow much of that normal daily move has already happened today. Over 100% means today is unusually wide
Where it isPlain tags such as "Above VWAP", "Above opening range" or "Heavy volume 2.5x"

Top gainers, Top losers and Most active show Yahoo's market-wide lists, with volume compared with an average day.

Levels for one stock​

Click any row to see its level ladder: yesterday's high, low and close, the premarket high and low, the opening range (the first 15 minutes), VWAP and today's high and low. They're sorted by price, with the current price highlighted, and each shows how far away it is.

These are the prices where other traders often react, so they're common places to plan entries, stops and targets.

Above the ladder is a chart of today in 5-minute bars, with premarket shaded, the VWAP line, the key levels as dashed lines and volume underneath. Hover over a bar to see its prices.

:::note Odd volume bars Yahoo's live volume figures sometimes contain a wildly wrong bar. Bars more than 10 times a normal bar are drawn faintly and capped, so they don't hide the rest, and they're left out of VWAP and relative volume. :::

Options for one stock​

Under the levels, US stocks get an Options panel. It looks at one idea: buying a call (gains if the stock rises) and a put (gains if it falls) at the same strike price. Holding both pays only if the stock makes a big move, either way, before the expiry date. Indian stocks don't have it, because Yahoo has no Indian option prices. The same panel opens from the Scanner's Options view.

  • Look ahead (1 to 8 weeks) and Strike price (the one closest to today's price comes first) choose what you see.
  • Four headline numbers for the chosen expiry date: what the call and put cost together in dollars, the prices where the pair pays at expiry, the move needed either way, and the time cost: roughly what the pair loses each day if the stock sits still.
  • What the option prices say: four short readings, each with a one-word answer:
    • Option prices (Pricey, About fair or Cheap): the swings option prices assume (implied volatility, IV) against how much the stock actually moved over the last 20 days.
    • Event ahead: when the nearest expiry is priced for bigger swings than later ones, news is usually due soon, often earnings. Option prices tend to drop right after it, even if the stock moves.
    • Protection against a fall: whether puts below the price cost more than calls above it.
    • Which way the pair leans: the call and put Delta added together, and how fast that changes as the stock moves (Gamma). These mostly follow the stock price; they don't predict it.
  • Compare expiry dates: a Simple table (cost, move needed, where it pays, time cost a day) or Detailed, which adds Delta, Gamma and IV. Click a date to see it in the headline numbers, the scenario and the history.
  • Try a scenario: type a stock move, a number of days, a change in IV and a number of pairs to see the estimated profit or loss, and a row of results from −10% to +10%. Try an IV change of −5 to see the drop that often follows earnings.
  • History: Yahoo keeps no history of these numbers, so the dashboard saves them once a day when you open the panel. From the next trading day you can see how Delta, Gamma, IV and the cost changed for the chosen strike and date.

Prices are the middle of Yahoo's bid and ask. Delta, Gamma, the time cost and the scenario come from a standard pricing model (Black-Scholes, without dividends), so treat them as a guide, not a quote. Outside market hours they're worked out from the last close. The panel describes what option prices imply; it isn't a buy or sell signal, and options can lose their whole cost.

Market calendar​

Some reports move the whole market at once. The Market calendar under the levels lists the ones coming up in the next 14 days for the US, India and the UK, in your own time zone:

  • Interest-rate decisions: the Fed, the Bank of England and the RBI
  • Inflation: CPI, and PCE (the one the Fed watches most)
  • Jobs and growth: the US jobs report, GDP, business surveys (ISM), retail sales and weekly jobless claims

Each event is marked Big mover, Can move or Minor, shows the last reading, and says in one line why it matters. Yahoo publishes several lines for one report (seven for CPI alone); they're merged into one event. Minor statistics are left out.

When a big event is due in the next 24 hours, a warning appears above your list: "Big news today: Fed interest-rate decision at 2:00 PM". Prices can jump in seconds around these, so many traders wait until the number is out, or trade smaller. Use the US, India and UK buttons to choose which countries you see; the dashboard remembers your choice.

Ask Claude too: what's on the economic calendar this week?

:::note Dates can move The calendar comes from Yahoo Finance and is refreshed every 6 hours. Dates occasionally change, and an event marked "Time not set" hasn't had its time announced yet. :::

Price alerts​

Click the bell next to any level to be alerted when the price reaches it, or add an alert by hand under Price alerts (symbol, above or below, price).

  • Alerts are checked every minute while the dashboard page is open, even in a background tab. Close the page and they pause.
  • The first time, your browser asks whether the page may show notifications. Allow it to get a desktop notification; otherwise you'll see a message on the page.
  • Each alert fires once. Press Clear fired to tidy the list.

Position size calculator​

Pick a symbol and the calculator fills in the current price as the entry. Choose Long or Short, then set a stop, either by typing it or by clicking one of the suggested levels (VWAP, opening range, premarket, yesterday, today's high or low, or one ATR away).

It shows:

  • Shares: how many you can buy so that hitting the stop loses only your risk per trade (for example 1% of your account).
  • Risk if stopped, position value and the share of your account it uses.
  • Targets at 1R, 2R and 3R: one, two and three times your risk, measured from the entry.

It warns you when the stop is very tight compared with the stock's normal daily move, and it cuts the size to what your account can pay for without margin.

Underneath, Before you place it is a short checklist. Some items are checked for you (within your limits, stop set, no big scheduled news such as a Fed decision in the next hour for that market, not chasing a stock that has already moved more than usual today); the others you tick yourself (you know your setup, you checked the company's news and earnings date, you'll respect the stop). It never blocks anything, since the tool doesn't place trades; it's a habit that catches common mistakes.

Today's limits​

Set your limits once under Your limits:

  • Account size
  • Risk per trade (% of the account)
  • Max daily loss (% of the account)
  • Max trades a day
  • Break after this many losses in a row

After each trade, log the result under Log a closed trade (symbol, long or short, and the profit or loss). Open More details to add the entry, exit, shares, your planned stop, the setup (for example "Opening range breakout" or "VWAP reclaim") and a note. With entry, exit and shares, the profit or loss is worked out for you; with the stop too, the result is also shown in R (multiples of the risk you planned). The Today's limits card then shows one of four states:

StatusWhen
Within your limitsEverything is fine
CautionHalf your daily loss limit is gone, or you have one trade left
Take a breakYou've lost several trades in a row
Stop for todayYou hit your daily loss limit or your trade limit

The pattern day trader rule​

The old US rule needed $25,000 in a margin account to make 4 or more day trades in 5 business days. US regulators replaced it in 2026, and brokers have until October 2027 to switch over. If your broker still applies the old rule, tick My broker still uses the old $25k pattern day trader rule. The card will then warn you at 3 day trades and stop you at 4. Check the details with your broker.

Day-trade journal​

In the Journal tab, switch to Day trades to review what you logged:

  • Net profit or loss, win rate, profit factor (total won ÷ total lost), average per trade, average R and average win and loss
  • What your log shows: plain observations, such as your best and weakest setups, your weakest time of day, or losses being bigger than wins
  • Running P/L by day as a line chart
  • Results by setup, by time of day (in the followed market's time) and by symbol
  • Every trade, with a button to remove one

Patterns need about 20 trades or more before they mean much.

Where it's saved​

Your limits are in ~/.stock-analyst/risk.json, logged trades in ~/.stock-analyst/daytrades.jsonl, alerts in ~/.stock-analyst/alerts.json and the options history in ~/.stock-analyst/options/ (one file per stock, kept 180 days). They stay on your computer and are never sent anywhere. Trade times are saved in the followed market's time (New York time, or IST for India) so they line up with the market day. Trades you logged before switching keep their original time.
