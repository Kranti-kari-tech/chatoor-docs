What's new

The latest changes, newest first. Already installed? Update in one command.

November 2026​

Version 0.19.0: a Mac app, and reports you can share​

  • A desktop app on Mac. Install on a Mac and you get a double-click Chatoor app that opens in its own window — no terminal, no browser tab — with the native menu bar (⌘Q to quit, Copy/Paste/Select All), the window reopening where you left it, and the trishul in the Dock. On a Mac that can't open a window, and on Windows and Linux, the icon opens Chatoor in your browser as before.
  • A shared report looks like Chatoor. The deep-dive report carries the trishul mark and "Chatoor by Krantikari" at the top of every page, and "Research tool, not financial advice" at the foot — so a screenshot you post anywhere is unmistakably Chatoor. Three sections now read in plain words: The case for and The case against (were Bull and Bear case), and What could change the picture (was Catalysts and dates). There's also a branded social card to screenshot and share — the mark, the company, the signals label and the one-line answer.

Version 0.18.0: Today, and news that may matter​

  • Today: open Chatoor and the first screen each day is a calm digest — the market, your stocks since their last scan, what's worth a look (ideas to review, results coming up, your paper account), news that may matter, the most traded stocks, and one idea to think about. Turn it off any time; it's always under the newspaper icon.
  • News ranked by what may matter to you, from free, open sources (Google News, the GDELT news index, the Federal Reserve, SEC filings). Each headline says in plain words why it might matter. It describes; it never says what to do.
  • Your profile (the person icon, top right): your own record — your calls next to the tool's, Rewind, paper trade, Trading desk habits — and this month against last. It reads only files on your computer.
  • A new logo: the Chatoor trishul, a C for Chatoor with a rising trade line.
  • The Scanner shows the latest prices on every scan, with a Refresh button and the time each price is from.
  • "Filter the stocks you follow" (was "Screen your stocks"): clearer name, with an example.
  • For study participants: send your data automatically each week, and see how you compare with other participants once the study ends.
  • A security fix, and a check that keeps the app's wording in a calm, teaching tone.

Version 0.17.1: the Trading desk​

  • The Day tab is now the Trading desk, right after Paper trade, since the lessons send you between the two. It's still off until you turn it on from Start.
  • The version you're running shows at the end of the line at the bottom of every page, for example "Chatoor 0.17.1". Mention it when you report something.
  • On a phone, tab names wrap onto two lines instead of being cut off.

Version 0.17.0: Chatoor, and day-trading lessons​

  • Sparring is now Chatoor, a play on chatur, Hindi for clever: the friend who always asks one more question. Your data, settings and the stock-analyst command are unchanged. Updating replaces the "Sparring" icon on your desktop with "Chatoor".
  • Strategy lessons on the Day tab: one day-trading strategy every two weeks (opening range breakout, VWAP, gaps, yesterday's high and low), each with when it applies, where it would enter, exit and take profit, and what goes wrong. Practise in Paper trade opens an order with the lesson attached, and the lesson shows your own results.
  • Lesson strategies on a stock: pick a stock on the Day tab to see which strategies its chart shows today, and how each did on it over the last 60 trading days, with how many trades that rests on. Most of them lose money more often than not, and the page says so.
  • The entry and exit rule only plans a pullback in an uptrend. On past prices, pullbacks without one lost money; with the change the rule is about break-even before costs, still a rule to think with rather than an edge.
  • The Journal's ideas table fits the screen again, and a young idea reads "leaning right" with "day 5 of 30" under it.

Version 0.16.1​

  • The first published 0.16 release (0.16.0 was held back by a test check). Everything below is in it.
  • A study code typed into the Journal is no longer wiped before you press Join the study on a slow computer.

Version 0.16.0: Paper trade, options in the Scanner, and the tool's own record​

Shaped by what early testers told us.

  • Paper trade has its own tab. Practise with pretend money, and see your tracked ideas next to your holdings: your view, the tool's view when you bought, and whether each is leaning right or wrong so far.
  • Buy calls and puts with pretend money. Switch the order panel to Options: before you buy it shows the cost, the price the stock must pass by expiry, the most you can lose and what you'd get selling now. Buys fill at the ask and sales at the bid, as at a real broker, and contracts settle after expiry.
  • Options in the Scanner. A Stocks | Options switch scans the same symbols for their options: the move needed by the next expiry, whether option prices look pricey or cheap, whether news looks due, and demand for protection. Click a card for the full options panel. It describes option prices; it isn't a buy or sell signal.
  • The tool's record in the Journal: every label the tool gave a symbol you scanned, checked 30 days later against its index, counting each symbol once a month so the record doesn't flatter itself. A published record from a fixed list of about 150 stocks starts collecting now.
  • The entry and exit rule, tested on past prices: across 150 large stocks and five years, the first target came before the stop about half the time, roughly break-even before costs, and better when the rule waited for a close above the 50-day average. The analysis panel now says how the setup it's showing has done.
  • Fewer tabs to start with. Start steps aside once you've done its first five steps (Learn links back to it), and Portfolio for your real holdings is off until you turn it on from Start, unless you already have holdings.

Version 0.15.0: options on the Day tab​

  • Options on the Day tab, for US stocks: what a call and a put at the same strike cost together over the next 1 to 8 weeks, where the pair pays at expiry, the move it needs and what it loses each day if the stock sits still. Four short readings say in plain words whether option prices look pricey or cheap next to how the stock has moved, whether news looks due soon, whether traders are paying for protection against a fall, and which way the pair leans (Delta and Gamma).
  • A Simple or Detailed table compares expiry dates, a scenario shows the profit or loss for a move you type, and a history shows how Delta, Gamma and IV changed day by day for the strike you chose. It's a description of what option prices imply, not a buy or sell signal.
  • The Day tab now refreshes everything every minute in the background, and Refresh fetches the latest prices from Yahoo straight away.

Version 0.14.0: a clearer Entry and exit, with a trend band​

  • Entry and exit reads the same way for every stock: a status (In the entry zone now, Waiting for a pullback, Waiting for a close above the 50-day average or No trade from this rule right now), a five-step trend band from Strong downtrend to Strong uptrend with a line on whether the data label agrees, the trend against its averages, what the rule is waiting for, then the plan.
  • After a steep fall or a fast rise (more than 15% from the entry), it says there's no trade yet and what would start one, with no stop or targets. It used to show a stop above the price. Tracking an idea then saves no levels.
  • When the rule waits for a falling or rising 50-day average, it shows where that average would be in 5 weeks if the price held.

Version 0.13.0: screens and what the price assumes​

  • Screen your stocks on the Scanner: filter the stocks you follow by the panel's own answers (the data label, the business, the price, the trend, how far below the 52-week high, RSI under 30, a report within 14 days). Save a screen to be told, while the dashboard is open, when a stock enters or leaves it.
  • What the price assumes in the analysis panel: works back from today's price to the yearly profit and sales the company would need in a few years, and the growth that takes, next to its past growth and analysts' forecast. Change the years, the P/E, the margin and the price growth you want. Arithmetic on your assumptions, never a fair value or target.

Version 0.12.0: peers and results days​

  • Against its peers in the analysis panel: the stock next to rivals you choose (or ones it suggests from stocks you follow in the same industry), with value over sales, forward P/E, sales growth, gross margin and 6-month return, the peers' middle value, and a plain line on where it stands. Deep dives use the same peers.
  • Results days: the Scanner lists the stocks you follow that report in the next two weeks. Before a report the panel shows The next report (what analysts expect and their range, how that forecast moved, the beat-and-miss record, typical results-day moves); for ten days after, The last report (earnings against the forecast and how the price reacted).
  • A forecast for a loss is described in words ("a loss, narrowed") instead of a percent change that could point the wrong way.
  • Opened the install link in a browser by mistake? The installer now says to paste the whole line into Terminal or PowerShell instead.

Version 0.11.1: more accurate numbers​

  • Cash now includes short-term investments, so "Can it pay its bills?" and a deep dive's net cash aren't understated for companies that keep money in them. Debt includes leases, and the panel says so.
  • A company that loses money is never called a "Strong" business; growth and cash make it "Mixed" at best.
  • Deep dives say when Yahoo is missing a quarter instead of leaving it out of charts, and chart values always show two decimals.

Version 0.11.0: Stock Analyst is now Sparring​

  • The app has a new name: Sparring by Krantikari. Updating replaces the desktop icon with one called Sparring; your data, the stock-analyst command and everything else stay as they were.
  • The first time it starts, the app asks for the email and key from your invitation. It checks the key online about once a day and keeps working for two weeks without internet. A key works on up to three computers.
  • The app asks once whether you'd like to share which tabs and features you use, to help decide what to improve. It's off unless you say yes, never includes your email, amounts, holdings or notes, and you can change your answer under How to use (the ? button). See Your data and privacy.

Version 0.10.0: deep dives that check themselves​

  • A deep dive's printable report is now built by one step that also checks every page fits on its sheet, so no page runs over in the PDF.

Version 0.9.4: the chart matches the price, and better deep dives​

  • During trading hours the analysis panel's chart and its "if the price holds" table now use the same up-to-date price as the headline, instead of one up to an hour old.
  • Deep dives come with two more charts drawn for you (the two-year what-if range and how analysts' estimates moved), and Claude checks that the price agrees on every page.
  • A day trade logged on a weekend, by the Indian market's clock, no longer disappears from the Day tab's list.

Version 0.9.3: easier to read, tidier on every screen​

  • Small grey text is darker, so it's easier to read in both light and dark mode.
  • On a tablet the top bar fits again, Scanner cards line up their buttons, the Day trading card spans its row, and phone controls share one left edge.

Version 0.9.2: Windows install fixes​

  • Updating on Windows while Stock Analyst is open now closes it first instead of failing halfway, and the desktop shortcut works for usernames with accented letters.
  • Opening several Learn entries quickly no longer loses one from your Start progress.
  • Fixes from writing an IREN deep dive: a company's backlog from SEC filings no longer mixes in one-off contract figures, "This setup before" keeps real big days instead of mistaking them for share splits, and former bitcoin miners filed under financial services (like IREN) get their debt and cash pictures again.

Version 0.9.1: a faster first scan​

  • On a new computer, the first scan used to take about 13 seconds while the app set itself up. It now gets ready in the background while the welcome screen shows, so the first scan takes about a second.

Version 0.9.0: from the company's filings​

  • For US companies, the analysis panel shows figures from their SEC reports that Yahoo doesn't have: the backlog of contracts booked but not yet delivered (and how many years of sales it equals), money customers paid ahead, and debt due in the next two years.
  • Claude's quick score now shows the same four questions and numbers as the dashboard panel.

Version 0.8.1: faster first opens​

  • After a scan, the dashboard fetches what each card's analysis panel needs in the background, so opening a card for the first time is quick. With --auto it does the same for your watchlist and holdings.

Version 0.8.0: what the panel said, and what followed​

  • Tracking an idea now saves what the analysis panel said about it. Once ideas are reviewed, the Journal shows, for each answer (like "The price: Low" or "The trend: Falling"), how many of those stocks beat their index. See Journal and reviews. It stays on your computer and isn't in the Research export.

Version 0.7.1: fixes from a consistency review​

  • Crypto, currencies and indexes no longer show company sections: they read "Not a company", with no dividend line.
  • "This setup before" leaves out falls caused by a share split Yahoo didn't adjust, and says so.
  • Debt is shown once, in "Can it pay its bills?". Amounts always carry their currency, dates read "Jun 30, 2026", and the trend card says Falling for a stock below both its averages before they cross.

Version 0.7.0: this setup before, and a two-year what-if​

  • This setup before: the analysis panel shows every past time the stock fell 40% or more below its high with RSI under 35, across all its price history, and what happened 3 and 12 months later. It says when there are too few cases to lean on.
  • Two years out, a what-if: next year's expected earnings at today's P/E, with the low and high estimates and two more variations, each showing its arithmetic. It isn't a forecast or a price target.

Version 0.6.0: the business in pictures​

  • The analysis panel now shows the business in four pictures for stocks: is it growing and keeping more, can it pay its bills, is the cash real, and is the margin rising. Each has a small chart and a one-phrase answer from a fixed rule. See The analysis panel.
  • Three new folded sections: Where each dollar goes, What you are paying (including how analysts' estimates moved against the price) and Cash and insiders.

Version 0.5.0: a clearer analysis panel, and printable deep dives​

  • Four questions first: the analysis panel answers the business, the price, the trend and what would change it in one word and one line each, then shows the numbers people ask about (dividend, buybacks or new shares, debt to profit, Rule of 40). See The analysis panel.
  • A year of the price with its 50-day and 200-day averages and their crosses, RSI and ADX gauges, and where the averages go if the price holds. Results days so far shows how the stock moved on each past results day.
  • Each fact once: repeated sections are merged, so the panel is shorter.
  • Printable deep dives: ask Claude for a deep dive and get a report of about 14 pages, saved as a PDF, with charts drawn from checked numbers and sources on every page. See Full reports.

Version 0.4.1: uninstall in one line​

  • To remove the dashboard, paste your install line with install changed to uninstall. It stops the app, removes it and its desktop icon, and asks before deleting your data. See Removing it.

Version 0.4.0: the entry and exit rule gets measured, and a one-line install​

  • Tracking an idea now saves its entry and exit levels. The Journal's new Rule column shows whether the price reached the first target or the stop first, and a line above the table adds them up. See Journal and reviews and The analysis panel.
  • Installing the dashboard is one pasted line, with no GitHub account or other setup. It adds a Stock Analyst icon to your desktop and opens the app. See Install the dashboard.
  • Prices under 1 (some crypto and penny stocks) keep their decimals.

Entry and exit levels in the analysis panel​

  • A new Entry and exit section shows where one fixed price rule would enter, where its stop is, and its targets, with how much each target could gain for every dollar at risk.
  • It warns when earnings fall inside the trade, when the score is negative, or when the stop is wide. It reads the price chart only and isn't advice. See The analysis panel.
  • The same levels appear in Claude's quick score (score.py).

Version 0.3.2: analysis panel fix​

  • In 0.3.0 and 0.3.1, Open analysis showed "Couldn't load" for any symbol with a saved full report. It opens again and shows the report.

October 2026​

Calmer Learn page and analysis panel​

  • Hover to preview: hover over any factor name or i icon to see the Learn card right there; click to open it on Learn.
  • Learn shows one topic at a time. Each card starts with one short sentence, ten have a small diagram, and More opens the full explanation.
  • The analysis panel says each thing once: a one-line summary, two diagrams (what pushed the score, and where the price is), one Watch out line, the case for and against, and the track record. Everything else is folded under More detail.

Version 0.3.1​

  • The first published 0.3 release (0.3.0 was held back by a test check). Everything below is in it.

Version 0.3.0​

  • Rewind, How sure vs. how right, the Indian market on the Day tab, a dashboard-only install, and a calmer Learn page and analysis panel. Details below.

Rewind: practise on the past​

  • A new Rewind tab: a real stock from one to four years ago, with its name and the date hidden. Make your call and how sure you are, then see what happened in seconds and what the tool would have said. Your record and How sure vs. how right build up as you play.

Day tab: Indian market​

  • A US / India switch on the Day tab. India shows the Nifty 50, Bank Nifty, India VIX and NSE sectors, runs the session clock, trade log and daily limits on IST, and hides the US-only lists and rule. Computers set to India time start on India.

Research export​

  • Taking part in a study? Research export at the bottom of the Journal saves an anonymous file of your learning data (your ideas, views, results and Look back choices, never your notes, holdings or name) for you to send to the researcher. You see all of it first.

Version 0.2.0: learn by deciding first​

  • Guess first on the Scanner hides the tool's scores until you've made your own call, so you find out whether your judgement adds anything. Tracking an idea no longer shows the tool's label before you choose; your call and the tool's appear side by side afterwards.
  • Look back in the Journal: once an idea is checked, say whether the result came from your reasons or from luck. Your answer is saved as a lesson.
  • Start's example companies follow your time zone (Reliance, TCS and HDFC Bank in India), and Learn covers Indian market hours.

Version 0.1.1: license and beta testers​

  • The skill now ships with its license inside, so every install and download includes it. Stock Analyst is in a private beta: if you've been invited, you may use it for your own testing only.
  • New beta testers' guide (TESTERS.md in the repository): setup, what to try and how to send feedback.

Version 0.1.0: the first release​

  • Releases now have version numbers. 0.1.0 is the first; until 1.0.0 the tool is in early development, and every release comes with notes on what changed. See Versions.

Market calendar​

  • Checked before a trade. "Before you place it" now flags big scheduled news for that stock's market in the next hour.
  • What's coming up. The Day tab lists the next 14 days of market-moving events for the US, India and the UK (rate decisions, inflation, jobs, GDP), each with why it matters, and warns you when one is due in the next 24 hours. See Day trading.

Tabs at the bottom on small screens​

  • On a phone-sized screen the tabs (Start, Scanner, Journal, Portfolio, Learn, and Day when it's on) sit in a bar at the bottom with icons, instead of being cut off at the top.

Simpler cards and analysis panel​

  • Plain words first. Each card shows up to three points like "Very profitable" or "Price trending down", with the seven factor bars behind Show the 7 factors (or tick Show factor details for every card).
  • 3 things to know. The analysis panel opens with what the data shows, the biggest strength and the biggest risk, then the track record and the bull and bear cases. The other sections are folded until you open them.

Practice mode and faster feedback​

  • Practice with pretend money. Portfolio → Practice gives you a pretend account (10,000 by default) that buys and sells at today's prices. It remembers what the tool said when you bought, compares you with the market since you started, and tells you plainly what your choices did. See Portfolio.
  • "So far" for young ideas. Tracked ideas under 30 days old show how they're doing so far ("day 5 · leaning right") instead of just "too early".

Tamper checks​

  • The dashboard checks on start that its files match the published version, and warns you if not. You can check by hand too: see Checking your copy.
  • The tools the scripts download are locked to exact versions with fingerprints, so a tampered download is refused.

Clear messages when something goes wrong​

  • Every empty page now explains what goes there and what to do next, with a small illustration. Every error looks the same and has Try again and Report a problem. Unknown addresses show a "This page doesn't exist" page.
  • Errors show a plain message where they happen, with no technical text on screen; the details go to a log file (~/.stock-analyst/logs/dashboard.log). A "The dashboard has stopped" page shows how to restart it and reconnects by itself.
  • A banner says when report formatting or icons couldn't load from the internet.
  • Report a problem is one click: it opens a GitHub issue with the title and technical details filled in. You add a line and press Submit on GitHub, and nothing personal is included. See Troubleshooting.

Analysis, not decisions​

  • New labels. Scores now read Positive signals, Mixed signals or Negative signals instead of BUY-lean, HOLD and SELL-lean. They describe the data; they aren't instructions. Old journal entries keep working.
  • Track record with every score. Each card and analysis panel shows how often the tool's past calls with that label turned out right, once at least 5 have been reviewed.
  • Welcome screen. The first time you open the dashboard, it explains what the tool can and can't do. Open it again any time with the ? button.
  • Checklists. A short checklist before you log a call, and a "Before you place it" checklist under the position size calculator.
  • Start here. New users land on a six-step path that ticks itself off as they go. Day trading tools are off until you turn them on.
  • Your own view. "Log call" is now Track idea: you say whether you expect the stock to beat, match or lag its index, and the Journal compares you with the tool.
  • "Data: complete / partial / thin" replaces "Confidence", which read as "the tool is sure".
  • Learn tab. Every number and label explained in plain English, searchable, and linked from the factor names and the small i icons across the dashboard.

Day tab for day traders​

A new Day tab in the dashboard shows today's key price levels (yesterday's high and low, premarket, opening range, VWAP), relative volume, daily range, the market and sectors, and Yahoo's top movers. A position size calculator and your own daily limits (loss limit, trade count, a break after losing streaks) help keep risk small. It also has a chart of today with VWAP and the levels, price alerts (with desktop notifications) and a Day trades journal with win rate, profit factor, results in R and breakdowns by setup and time of day. See Day trading tab.

Free AI reports, a local database and one-command mode​

  • Write report with free AI: a button in the dashboard's analysis panel writes a full report using a free Google Gemini, Groq or OpenRouter key. It falls back to the next provider if one is busy. Only public market data is sent; your holdings and journal stay on your computer. See Free AI reports.
  • Local database: fetched prices, company data and headlines are saved in ~/.stock-analyst/cache.db, so repeat views are instant and still work when Yahoo is slow. A daily score history builds up for every stock you look at.
  • One command: dashboard.py --open --auto keeps your portfolio and watchlist updated every 30 minutes and writes missing reports automatically.

Demo video and security checks​

  • A 21-second demo of the dashboard is now on the home page.
  • Dependencies are up to date: Python scripts now need yfinance 1.7+ and pandas 3.1+ (uv installs them automatically, with Python 3.11 or newer), and the dashboard uses the latest marked and DOMPurify.
  • Automatic vulnerability checks run on every change and every week. Dashboard libraries are pinned with integrity hashes, so a tampered file won't load.
  • Dashboard polish: the journal shows how many calls are logged before the first review, and the first scan's results no longer sit flush against the search box.

Portfolio tab in the dashboard​

Add, view and remove holdings in the dashboard. It shows totals per currency, an allocation bar, profit and loss, a warning when one holding gets too big, and today's score for each holding. Click a holding for its full analysis. The buttons, dropdowns and focus outlines across the dashboard were also tidied up.

Analysis panel in the dashboard​

Click any card in the dashboard to open a full analysis:

  • Why it's moving: trend, recent returns, distance from the high, RSI and upcoming earnings, in plain words
  • Latest headlines from the past 7 days, with links
  • Bull case and bear case, built from what is helping and hurting the score
  • What I'd watch: price levels and dates that would change the picture
  • Cautions and gaps in this research
  • Trading practice notes, for learning only
  • Claude's full report, if you've asked for one

Full reports are now saved automatically to ~/.stock-analyst/reports/, so the panel can show them.

Dashboard​

A local web page to scan symbols, see the results as cards, keep a watchlist, log calls and follow your journal. See Dashboard.

ETF support​

ETFs such as GLD, SPY, TLT and NIFTYBEES.NS are detected automatically and scored on fund factors: momentum, track record, risk, cost and liquidity. Each is compared with the right index for what it holds; gold is judged on its own return. See ETFs and funds.

First release​

Quick scores, full reports, comparisons, a portfolio tracker, and a journal that learns from its past calls.
